# Discussion (thesis draft)

*Governed by `synthesis/SCIENTIFIC_CLAIM_LADDER.md`. Chang / Newell / Verrel used only as theoretical context — not to reshape empirical claims.*

---

## A. Main finding

Across four within-participant case studies, reference-anchored JcvPCA showed **participant-specific longitudinal redistribution** of body-link contributions to whole-body movement structure at T1→T2. Selected links exceeded each participant’s own T1 R1–R2 signed natural variability with direction stability (A2). Anatomical patterns differed across participants. This is the primary scientific result.

A3 (broader underused-link participation / reweighting language under the full S3 stack plus supervisor sign-off) remains **not formally supported**. Step-5 “organization” labels (including the 51-organization count) are descriptive mechanism classifications and must not be equated with A3.

---

## B. Meaning of the R1/R2 (within-session repetition) reference

Comparing longitudinal change to within-session repetition variability strengthens interpretation by asking whether observed T1→T2 change exceeds the participant’s own short-term repetition difference under the **same method**. For JcvPCA, this is operationalized as signed NV with an anchor-swap direction gate (S2/A2). Parallel, method-specific Drep quantities play an analogous role for Conv, features, RQA, and coupling.

Limitations of this reference should be stated clearly:

- R1/R2 is not a complete noise floor or between-day variability estimate;  
- only two repetitions are available;  
- the JcvPCA A2 floor is a single matched pair (no bootstrap population inference);  
- method-specific Drep values are **not** numerically interchangeable across methods.

Nevertheless, the framework is well matched to N-of-1 improvisational data where group means would obscure participant structure.

---

## C. Coordination reorganization and existing coupling

JcvPCA and existing coupling measure different constructs. JcvPCA indexes redistribution of **link contributions to whole-body structure**. Existing coupling indexes **dynamic relationships between regional movement-intensity (speed-magnitude) signals**. They are not mathematically equivalent, and speed-magnitude coupling must not be narrated as Chang-style anatomical in-phase/anti-phase joint coupling.

The coupling audit shows **heterogeneous reorganization**:

- some relationships **strengthen** beyond R1/R2 (notably 651–ex13 trunk–arm lagged coupling ↑; 671–ex12 trunk–arm ↑);  
- some **weaken** (e.g., 671–ex10 regional coupling ↓; selected decreases in 252/790);  
- some remain **stable** despite strong JcvPCA redistribution (671–ex09).

This pattern argues against any thesis sentence of the form “learning = universally reduced coupling” or “learning = universally increased coupling.” Preferred interpretation:

> Coordination adaptation in these data can involve heterogeneous redistribution and reorganization rather than a uniform increase or decrease in coupling or dimensionality.

Theoretical context (not data fitting): Chang et al. noted that local coupling and whole-body PCA organization can diverge; Newell & Vaillancourt argued that dimensional/DOF change has no universal direction under task constraints; Verrel et al. distinguish task-variable stabilization (not measured here) from DOF use/coordination. These references support caution; they do not license Bernstein-stage claims in this dataset.

**Claim status:** mixed coupling change beyond R1/R2 is **consistent with** participant-specific motor adaptation / coordination reorganization — **not proof** of motor learning or DOF unfreezing.

## D. Cross-method triangulation

Methods were assigned roles (detection, anatomical localization, exercise localization, interpretation, temporal characterization) and compared for convergence, complementarity, and discordance — not ranked as competing models and not counted as votes.

| Participant | Role in the narrative |
|---|---|
| **651** | Strongest multi-method convergence at D12: JcvPCA A2 (4/10 with exclusions), strong Conv >Drep, entropy/dimensionality features, RQA complementary ex13, and robust trunk–arm coupling ↑ at ex13. Compatible evidence that change involved both whole-body link redistribution and altered trunk–arm intensity coordination — **not** identical constructs. |
| **671** | JcvPCA-dominant evidence (A2/S3/persistence/rep_pass) with complementary mixed coupling reorganization and **limited** Conv corroboration. Shows that strong whole-body redistribution need not imply strong latent-model detection or a uniform coupling direction. |

## E. Motor adaptation versus motor learning

**Strongest defensible statement:**

> The findings are consistent with participant-specific longitudinal motor adaptation / coordination reorganization, in the sense that selected JcvPCA link-contribution changes — and, in selected cases, regional coupling and latent/feature descriptors — exceed each participant’s own within-session repetition variability under method-specific references.

**Why this does not prove motor learning:**

- N = 4 case studies, no group inference;  
- session–timepoint confounding;  
- repeated guided improvisation without a causal comparator at this stage;  
- limited repetition sampling;  
- no verified task-variable performance criterion (Verrel layer absent);  
- no demonstrated retention/transfer design sufficient for a learning claim.

---

## F. What is not supported

Current evidence does **not** support:

- shared whole-cohort direction of change;  
- global DOF unfreezing;  
- universal decrease or increase in coupling;  
- global increase in motor flexibility;  
- causal Gaga effect;  
- psilocybin effect on coordination;  
- amplitude-independent whole-body reorganization as a formal A3 claim (unless separately supervisor-approved);  
- “better learning” from ↑ or ↓ dimensionality/entropy;  
- equivalence of JcvPCA redistribution with stronger local joint coupling.

---

## G. Methodological contribution

Within this dataset, a participant-specific **JcvPCA + within-session repetition-reference** framework provided anatomically localized longitudinal evidence for complex whole-body improvisational movement, with complementary layers from Conv (detection/exercise localization), explicit features and regional coupling (descriptive biomechanics/coordination dynamics), and limited RQA temporal characterization. This is a contribution to analytical practice for N-of-1 complex movement data; it is **not** claimed as general method validation beyond these participants and exercises.

---

## Closing interpretation (Discussion only)

Taken together, the data support a picture of **participant- and exercise-specific coordination reorganization**: whole-body link-contribution structure can redistribute beyond repetition variability, while regional intensity-coupling relationships may strengthen, weaken, or remain stable depending on participant and exercise. That heterogeneity is itself a scientific result — not a failure to find a single universal learning signature.
